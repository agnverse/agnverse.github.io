AGNverse

Erciyes University Active Galaxies Research Group

As the Erciyes University AGN Research Group, we investigate active galactic nuclei (AGN) across X-ray, ultraviolet, optical, infrared, and radio wavelengths. By analyzing observational data across these regimes, we aim to uncover the structure, evolution, and host-galaxy interactions of AGNs. Our research integrates advanced statistical methods, machine learning, and scientific software development. The overarching goal of our work is to understand supermassive black hole growth, galaxy evolution, and energetic astrophysical systems throughout the cosmos. Utilizing data from XMM-Newton, Chandra, NuSTAR, XRISM, SDSS, LAMOST, LoTSS, RACS, and MeerKAT, we resolve the temporal and spectral properties of active galactic nuclei.

Research Vision

The primary mission of the AGNverse research group is to advance our understanding of supermassive black hole growth, galaxy evolution, and energetic astrophysical systems across the Universe. Combining multi-wavelength observations, statistical methods, and scientific software development, we investigate the fundamental physical processes driving Active Galactic Nuclei and extreme cosmic phenomena. We believe modern astronomy progresses through the seamless synergy of observational data, computational modeling, and open science.

Research Areas

Our core research areas, encompassing completed and ongoing publications, scientific projects, and thesis investigations:

Active Galactic Nuclei

Inner structure, evolution, variability characteristics, radio emission, giant radio quasars, and candidate binary supermassive black holes.

Quasar Winds

Broad absorption line quasars (BALQSOs), gas outflows, mass-loss mechanisms, and galactic-scale feedback processes.

Multi-Wavelength Astrophysics

Multi-band observations spanning optical, ultraviolet, X-ray, and radio regimes to decipher energetic astrophysical systems.

Astrostatistics

Statistical inference, uncertainty quantification, Bayesian modeling, and advanced analysis of large astronomical datasets.

Machine Learning

Deep classification, anomaly detection, feature extraction, and AI-driven big data analytics for astronomical observations.

Scientific Software Development

Reproducible scientific workflows, open-source astronomy tools, and high-performance computing in Python, MATLAB, and R.

Projects and Peer-Reviewed Publications

Scientific research projects focused on active galactic nuclei, supermassive black holes, and multi-wavelength astrophysics, alongside articles published in internationally recognized peer-reviewed journals.

Research Opportunities

We firmly believe that open science, reproducible research, and collaborative discovery are vital for the future of astrophysics. The AGNverse research group warmly welcomes all researchers—including undergraduate and graduate students, as well as institutional or independent scholars—seeking to collaborate on active galactic nuclei, astrophysics, and data-driven methods.

Undergraduate & Graduate Students

Research projects (e.g., TÜBİTAK 2209), senior capstone theses, and Master's / Ph.D. dissertation supervision. Hands-on experience with multi-wavelength astronomical datasets, machine learning pipelines, and computational modeling.

Postdoctoral & Independent Researchers

Collaborative research, joint publications, and hosting support for national and international postdoctoral fellowships (e.g., MSCA, TÜBİTAK 2218). Conduct high-impact astrophysics research utilizing our observational and computational resources.

Academic Collaboration & Joint Projects

Partnerships with faculty, professors, and researchers from other institutions worldwide. Joint telescope observing proposals (TUG, ESO, Chandra, XMM-Newton), data consortium initiatives, and co-authored scientific studies.
